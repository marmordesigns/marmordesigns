Understanding Stone Grades: What Premium Really Means

In the stone market, the word "Premium" is often used loosely. But what separates commercial builder-grade stone from true Gallery Grade architectural slabs? Here is an insider's guide to how stone quality is classified.

1. The Four Crucial Grading Metrics

When our stone inspectors evaluate newly sliced slabs in Kishangarh, they measure four distinct parameters:

  • Structural Soundness: Complete absence of structural dry seams, fissures, or weak calcium veins that crack under transit or fabrication.
  • Color & Mineral Uniformity: Consistent crystal distribution without unwanted rust patches or dark mineral blotches.
  • Dimensional Calibration: True planar flatness and uniform 18mm or 20mm thickness across all four corners.
  • Crystalline Depth: The three-dimensional translucency and light reflectivity of the natural quartz grains.

2. The Three Architectural Grades

Commercial Grade (Builder Grade)

Often sold at heavy discounts in unorganized stockyards. These slabs carry natural fissures filled with colored putty, varying thickness (16mm–19mm on the same slab), and high risk of edge chipping during CNC mitering.

Standard Architectural Grade

Uniformly cut with sound structural density and acceptable polish. Suitable for standard residential flooring and utility countertops.

Marmor Gallery Grade (Top 5% Cut)

The highest echelon of geological extraction. Zero micro-fractures, calibrated 20mm diamond edges, optical translucency, and book-matched veining. Hand-selected for luxury residences and hotels.
