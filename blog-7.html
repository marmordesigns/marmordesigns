How to Care for and Maintain Natural Stone Surfaces

Natural stone is one of the most durable materials on Earth, but preserving its diamond polish and crystalline depth requires the right daily care rituals.

1. The pH-Neutral Cleaning Rule

The single most common mistake homeowners make is using acidic bathroom cleaners or harsh vinegar-based sprays on natural stone. Acid reacts chemically with calcium and mineral binders, causing dull etch marks that require diamond buffing to remove.

Always clean quartz, agate, and rare stones with warm water and a few drops of mild pH-neutral liquid soap, followed by a wipe-down with a dry microfiber cloth.

✓ DO

  • Wipe spills (oil, wine, turmeric) promptly with a damp microfiber cloth.
  • Use silicone trivets under extremely hot cast-iron cookware.
  • Use cutting boards rather than slicing directly on stone.

✗ DON'T

  • Never use bleach, ammonia, or abrasive scrubbing powders.
  • Avoid placing acidic lemon halves or vinegar directly on unsealed stone.
  • Never use steel wool or metal scrapers on polished surfaces.

2. Sealing Schedules

While engineered Quartz is non-porous and never requires sealing, natural translucent stones like Onyx or porous granites benefit from an impregnating nano-penetrating sealer applied once every 12 to 18 months.
